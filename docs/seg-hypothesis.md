# Searl Effect Generator (SEG) Hypothesis — Controlled Test Protocol

## Position for Rev. C

The Searl Effect is treated as an **experimental hypothesis**, not as an established energy-generation mechanism.

Historical reports and prototype claims exist. Reproducible, high-confidence anomalous energy production under controlled conditions has not been demonstrated to the satisfaction of standard engineering standards. Therefore the SEG module must stand or fall on its own measured data.

## Required Measurement

For every test condition record:

\[
P_{\text{electrical,out}}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
P_{\text{mechanical,in}} + P_{\text{electrical,in}}
\]

with calibrated, traceable instrumentation (torque, speed, voltage, current, power analyzers).

## Minimum Control Set

1. Rollers / magnetic bearings installed (nominal configuration)
2. Rollers removed
3. Magnets demagnetized or replaced with non-magnetic equivalents (reference)
4. Sweep of rotational speeds
5. Sweep of magnetic field strengths (if adjustable)
6. Sweep of electrical load

## Decision Rule

- If net electrical output exceeds total measured input *and* the excess survives the full control set → the hypothesis becomes scientifically interesting and further investigation is justified.
- If the excess disappears under any of the controls → the effect is not anomalous under the conditions tested.

No design claim of “free energy” or over-unity performance is permitted until the above protocol has been executed and the data published in the `experiments/seg/` directory.
